This chapter grew out of conversations with retailers about the complexity of Pharmacy Benefit Manager (PBM) contracts and their uncertainty around third-party payments. I study how the aggregate nature of PBM contracts with retailers affects retailers' multiproduct ordering decisions relative to individual payment contracts.

Using a 2x2 laboratory newsvendor experiment, I compare individual and aggregate payment contracts to understand retailers' ordering behavior under demand uncertainty. In the first experiment, I find that aggregate contracts led to weaker overall performance and stronger pull-to-center behavior, while individual contracts were more exposed to demand uncertainty. A second experiment, designed around these results, will examine the underlying behavioral mechanisms.
