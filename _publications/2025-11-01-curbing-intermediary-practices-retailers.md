Using richer five-state Medicaid prescription claims data (over 30 million claims per state per year), I examine how curbing intermediary practices affected retailers' product mix, the quality of dispensed drugs, and the extent to which payer-side cost changes were ultimately passed through to retailers. This study extends the Chapter 1 (job market paper) investigation to the retailer side, and provides a foundation for continued work on how regulations governing intermediary practices affect different aspects of patient care: cost, quality, and access.
